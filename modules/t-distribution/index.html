    How the five Excel functions fit together
    T.DIST(t, df, TRUE)
    Signed t cutoff → area to its left.
    T.DIST.RT(t, df)
    Signed t cutoff → area to its right.
    T.DIST.2T(c, df)
    Nonnegative magnitude c → combined area below −c and above c.
    T.INV(p, df)
    Left-tail probability → signed t cutoff. A probability below 0.5 gives a negative cutoff.
    T.INV.2T(p, df)
    Combined two-tail probability → positive critical magnitude. For middle 95%, pass 0.05.

    TRUE gives cumulative probability; FALSE gives density height, not area. Since T is continuous, P(T = t) = 0.

    The standard Student t distribution is symmetric about zero. Its mean is undefined at df = 1; its variance is undefined at df ≤ 2. Larger df bring it closer to the standard normal curve. There is no separate mean or standard-deviation input.

    Excel truncates noninteger df; this explorer requires whole-number df so its displayed formulas use exactly the same model. A right-tail probability above 0.5 has a negative cutoff; a two-sided critical magnitude is always nonnegative.
