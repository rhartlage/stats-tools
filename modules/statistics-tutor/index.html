    The investigation

    Sampling conditions for this exercise

    The sample is randomly selected from a population much larger than the sample. Observations are independent. Measurements come from a roughly normal population with no extreme outliers. The population standard deviation is unknown. These are assumptions of the exercise, not facts established by n alone.

    CHANGE ONE THING AT A TIME

    What makes an interval wider?

    Predict first, then move a control. The sample mean stays at 50. This is a comparison of formulas with other inputs held fixed, not a new sample.

    x̄ ± t* × s / √n

    • Larger n → narrower interval, other inputs fixed.
    • Larger s → wider interval.
    • Higher confidence → wider interval.

    Precision is not the same as accuracy. A narrow interval from biased sampling can still miss the population mean.

    A FIXED MEAN. CHANGING INTERVALS.

    What does confidence look like?

    Take independent random samples from a normal population with fixed μ = 50 and σ = 10. Every sample gets its own x̄, s, and t interval. Predict: will every interval contain 50?

    Solid line + dot: contains μ. Dashed line + ×: misses μ. The chart shows the latest 30 intervals; the count includes every sample in this run. Nothing runs automatically.

    Over many repetitions, coverage approaches the confidence level under these assumptions. A batch can be above or below that level. Once an interval is observed, it either contains the fixed population mean or it does not.
